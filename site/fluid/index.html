Drag across the tank to push the fluid. This is Pavel Dobryakov’s WebGL Fluid Simulation running on your graphics card — the same family of maths that moves Earth’s air and oceans.

What you are watching

Advect, project, swirl

Every frame the simulation repeats three steps on a grid of cells. Advection carries dye and velocity along the flow. Projection solves a pressure field so the fluid neither piles up nor leaves holes — it stays incompressible. Vorticity confinement puts back the small eddies that a coarse grid would smear out.

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p/ρ + ν∇²u + f,   ∇·u = 0

The cyclone buttons spin the fluid the way the Coriolis effect spins real storms: anticlockwise in the northern hemisphere, clockwise in the southern. The trade-wind and jet-stream buttons push steady bands, like the easterlies near the equator and the westerly jet at mid-latitudes.
